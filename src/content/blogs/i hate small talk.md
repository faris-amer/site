# i hate small talk
i mean, it's all so tiresome, talking for the sake of talk. I want to talk about *real things*. talk about abstract topics like free will, and fate, and analyze the social circles around us, the childhood memories we've tried to forget and the adult ones we're looking forward to remembering. You know that's the stuff that I really enjoy talking about. small talk is just so dull. plus, I can never really get to know someone that way - who cares what their favorite color is or what last TV show they've watched half-asleep on the sofa, everyone knows what makes a person unique is all the stuff deep down, the real them. Conversations should be like sparring, where you take turns showing off new techniques you've learned and one-upping each other, both growing in the process. 
You should live to learn and improve yourself - everything else is just fluff in the way. i realize saying it out loud like this makes me seem insufferable, and, well,

A few years ago, I was surrounded by people I didn't know, trying to make a friend. Everyone's going around talking about what they're studying and how much they're stressed for midterms but that's not good enough for me, no, I need to create a genuine connection. I need to find someone who I really *get*, and who really *gets* me. All this fluff just isn't my style. And so I sat around and listened to the people tell stories of their dorms and how their roommate's toothpaste stains the sink purple and I listen along and nod and laugh when it's appropriate and talk when it's appropriate and say something about needing to catch a bus before heading out for the night.

I brush my teeth, noting the lack of purple in the sink and then lay in bed and chat with my friend, my real, true, close friend. He tells me of the people he's met over where he is, about one's habit of leaving sticky notes everywhere and another's casual softball league. He went on and on about his new friend group and all sorts of things he'd learned about them. He'd referred to them as friends, but I knew that really they're only friendly peers - it's impossible to make a friend in one day after all, he's only made small talk with them. Everyone knows you're only real friends when you big talk together. 

I tell him I'd met some interesting people but haven't really *connected* with any of them. \
He tells me I'm bound to find friends eventually. \
I tell him I hate making small talk. \
He says something about how i needed to lower my standards for people and force myself to be a real person. \
I don't understand - it often feels like I'm the only real one here anyways.

I complain some more, and then they tell me:

*"small talk is a beautiful thing. we need small talk. we need seemingly meaningless fleeting shallow relationships with people you've only really properly spoken to once or twice. they're not meaningless they are what being a human is all about.
trust me"*

I don't really understand. but I say something like yeah man you're right and I roll over and go to sleep

A few months later, we three (there's 3 now) decide we want to go on a pilgrimage to the grand chicago bean. I'm sitting in the passenger seat, and within an hour there's a loud snore coming from behind me. I look and my friend's passed out, mouth wide open. crap, I've got to keep the driver awake for the next 5 hours somehow - but man i hate small talk. We've never really been super close, always glued together by the snorer in the back. He tries to start some conversation and we talk about nothing for about 15 minutes before we decide that it's time to play some music.

We stop near the border to refill gas and get our passports in check, and then we swap, me in the back this time. For the rest of the ride, it's as if we'd all gotten a new breath of life. We talked about games and how Bo Burnham isn't as funny anymore and my friend's grandma and his sister and brother and how he's sad his father's moving away but hopeful for his graduate studies and me and the driver would interject with our own anecdotes - he'd always listen and relate with his own story or ask further, talking to him was the easiest thing in the world. But it was strange, even though the 3 of us were talking, it felt like me and the driver were having a conversation through the other friend, like there was an invisible wall between us. I didn't hate him or anything, and I'd tried really hard to get close with him in the past, but at this point I'd accepted that not everyone's going to be ecstatic about you, and that's alright.

We made many memories on that trip, but the drive there always stuck with me.

About a year later, i sent a message to a friend i knew a few years before that, but hadn't talked to in a while. We exchanged hellos and after asking if anything had happened, they said it'd be best if we didn't talk amymore.

They called me later that night, and I picked up, and we talked about performative arabs setting their clocks to arab numerals and a terrible burger and how they'd been making fufu once a week and how chicken hearts and tabasco go really well together. We talked about how they were going to have a terrible day tomorrow and then we talked about nothing for a while more, but it was getting late, so we agreed to talk again in a year.

it's strange how small talk can make up such a big part of your life. I'm still no good at it, but I make conversation with the old ladies in the elevator and equally energetic strangers at concerts and workers in the 8am tim horton's line and it's added a lot to the way I see others, in a really good way. It's made me really excited to go out into place with crowds where lots of people will be, and it's made me a bit sad about how we're all online now, and working from home has only made this clearer to me. "Can you hear me?" and "Can you see my screen?" have become the new "hey, how's your day?" and it's not doing us any favors. So I hate small talk - how scarce it's become, and how much I took it for granted.
